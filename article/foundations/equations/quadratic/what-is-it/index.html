Complicated square

We solve almost all elementary equations by simplifying them step by step until we get a trivial equality of the form x = A or A = x (which is the same thing), where A is some number that is the solution to the equation. It might seem that now we are all-powerful and can solve any equations at all! Well, let's check it out!

Equations with a nuance…

Applied
👀
Example
An ancient Greek king (they were called basileus) ordered a luxurious garden to be built with an area of 36 square meters. At the same time, one of its sides must be 5 meters longer than the other. What should the length and width of the garden be?
Ancient sages are confused…
As you can see, such equations occur regularly. This is not some unique case. Such equations, in which the unknown is raised to the second power, are found everywhere. It is not good when damn gardens, picture frames and simple motion problems can break our equations and lead us into a dead end! We urgently need to figure out how to solve such equations!

Quadratic trinomial

To solve such equations, we must first understand what we are dealing with. It seems obvious to consider equations that have x2x^2x2 as quadratic. But, as always, the most obvious solution may not be the best one. This approach has serious drawbacks:

    There may be no square, but the equation is still quadratic!
    In the examples about the garden and the picture frame above, we have already seen that an equation initially without any squares can turn out to be quadratic, but after transformations they appear.

Our enemy is cunning and treacherous! Quadratic equations can mimic, hiding their square, or they can have an explicit square and not be quadratic at all! But mathematicians are no fools either and were able to figure the way to determine exactly which equations are quadratic and which are not.
As in any good detective story, first mathematicians analyzed all equations and identified a “modus operandi”, a general portrait that is characteristic of all quadratic equations. They quite vulgarly called this portrait a quadratic trinomial.

  • Why quadratic?
    Because the maximum power of the variable in it is equal to two, that is, “variable squared”.
  • Why trinomial?
    Because it consists of three terms (monomials): Ax2Ax^2Ax2, Bx and C. And even if there are fewer of them, as for example in x2+3x^2 + 3x2+3, the missing term can be considered equal to zero: x2+0x+3x^2 + 0x + 3x2+0x+3.
  • Why A≠0A \neq 0A=0?
    Because if A = 0, x2x^2x2 multiplied by it becomes zero and disappears. And the polynomial is no longer quadratic! There is no “square”, that is, the second power, in it.

Quadratic equation

Quadratic trinomials are a general name for mathematical expressions of a certain type. They are sometimes used on their own, but now it is important that with their help we can formulate a solid and clear definition of quadratic equations.

Quadratic equation

The general form of a quadratic equation is any equation in which there is a quadratic trinomial on one side and zero on the other:
Ax2+Bx+C⏞Quadratic trinomial=0⏟Quadratic equation,A≠0\underbrace{\overbrace{Ax^2 + Bx + C}^{\text{Quadratic trinomial}} = 0}_{\text{Quadratic equation}}, \quad A \neq 0Quadratic equationAx2+Bx+C​Quadratic trinomial​=0​​,A=0
Any equation that has this general form or can be reduced to it by transformations without changing the roots is called a quadratic equation.

Power matters, not position

The vast majority of beginners get confused when determining the coefficients A, B and C in a quadratic equation. Coefficients are attached to x and its power. But the positions where they stand do not matter!
  1. 1
    Coefficient A always stands next to x2x^2x2.
  2. 2
    Coefficient B always stands next to x.
  3. 3
    Coefficient C always stands alone. There are no variables next to it!
Consider the example −3+4x2−2x=0-3 + 4x^2 - 2x = 0−3+4x2−2x=0. Remember that A is always before x2x^2x2, so it is equal to 4. Bis always before x, so it is equal to –2. –3 stands alone, this is the coefficient C.
Now we know the enemy by sight and understand that the coefficients do not depend on the position in the equation, but on what power of x they are next to. Let's practice determining whether equations are quadratic and finding their coefficients A, B and C.

Beginners and people who generally poorly understand this topic regularly get confused and use the terms “quadratic trinomial” and “quadratic equation” as synonyms. Just in case, let's clarify once again. A quadratic trinomial and a quadratic equation are NOT the same thing at all, but completely different mathematical objects! A quadratic trinomial is simply an expression of a certain type that can (and will later) be used for a wide variety of purposes:
